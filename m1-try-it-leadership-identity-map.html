Part 1 of 5

About this activity

You have spent the first half of this module looking at culture, identity, and bias. Now bring the lens directly to yourself.

This is a private Leadership Identity Map. The goal is not to reduce yourself to a set of labels. It is to notice which parts of your identity, values, and lived experience are active in the way you lead.

What you'll do

Move through six parts of your map, then look across it for a pattern. Write only what feels useful to you. Nothing needs to be submitted or shared.

Your writing stays in this browser. Nothing here is submitted, transmitted, or visible to a facilitator or anyone in your cohort. Your entries are kept on this device only, and you can download or clear them at any time.

If you are on a shared or public computer, use Clear my responses before you leave the page.

There is no right amount to write. A few words in a box is a complete answer.

Part 2 of 5

Where you come from

The first three parts of your map are about what shaped you before you ever led anything.

Map part 1

Identities and communities

Which identities, communities, cultures, or groups have shaped how you understand yourself and the world?

Map part 2

Roles and responsibilities

Which roles have shaped you most? Consider roles inside and outside education, including family, community, professional, or caregiving roles.

Map part 3

Formative experiences

Which experiences have strongly influenced what you believe about authority, fairness, conflict, success, belonging, or opportunity?

Part 3 of 5

What you carry in

The next three parts are about what is active when you are the one deciding.

Map part 4

Values and beliefs

Which values show up most clearly in the way you lead? Which values become harder to hold onto when you are under pressure?

Map part 5

Sources of expertise

Whose advice do you instinctively trust? What credentials, communication styles, experiences, or forms of knowledge tend to increase someone's credibility in your eyes?

Map part 6

Comfort and discomfort

Which people, situations, communication styles, or kinds of conflict feel easiest for you to navigate? Which tend to create tension, defensiveness, urgency, or avoidance?

Part 4 of 5

Notice the pattern

Look back across your map. You can reopen any part from the progress trail, or switch to All parts on one page to see everything at once.

Consider:

  • Which parts of your story are most visible in your leadership?
  • Which are active but rarely named?
  • Which experiences have shaped what you consider “professional,” “respectful,” “prepared,” or “capable”?
  • Where might a strength or value you rely on also narrow what you notice?

Complete one sentence:

Because of what I have experienced, I may be quicker to notice and slower to notice .

Part 5 of 5

Before you move on

Do not try to fix anything yet. Choose one pattern from your map that you want to keep in mind during Apply It.

Then ask yourself: how might this part of my perspective shape the story I tell when a leadership situation is ambiguous?
