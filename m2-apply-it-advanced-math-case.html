Apply It, 10 minutes

A Leadership Decision: Advanced Math Placement

About this application

Try It asked you to reframe a real pattern from your own context. Apply It asks you to practice making a leadership decision when the evidence is incomplete and the first explanation is not enough.

There is no score. The purpose is to make your assumptions, evidence choices, and tradeoffs visible.

The leadership situation

You are a district leader reviewing participation in advanced middle school mathematics.

The district serves a diverse student population, but enrollment in advanced math does not reflect that diversity. Some student groups are consistently underrepresented.

The current process uses standardized test scores, prior grades, teacher recommendations, and family opt-in. Advanced courses are offered at every middle school, but transportation, scheduling, and staffing differ by building.

At a leadership meeting, several explanations surface quickly:

  • “Some students simply are not ready for the pace.”
  • “Families have to choose the opportunity.”
  • “Teachers know which students can handle the course.”
  • “We use the same criteria for everyone.”

The superintendent wants a recommendation before the next cabinet meeting.

What you’ll do

Work through four decisions. At each one, choose the response closest to what you would do as the leader. You’ll see what that decision centers, what it risks overlooking, and which lens from the module it draws on. You can look at what the other responses would have done before moving on.

Nothing is submitted, and the activity never asks for your name, your district, or any student information. Your four selections are kept only in this browser so you can leave and come back. On a shared computer, use Clear my responses at the end.
