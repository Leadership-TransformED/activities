Try It, 10 minutes

Trace the Pattern Backward

About this activity

You have examined historical context through policy, place, and the experiences of educators and communities. Now shift from studying history to investigating the system you lead.

This activity asks you to trace one real pattern backward. The goal is not to prove exactly where the pattern came from. The goal is to challenge explanations your organization may have accepted too quickly.

What you’ll do

Return to the pattern you identified at the beginning of the Read section. Work through three lenses:

  • Policy and Structure
  • Place and Access
  • People and Knowledge

Part 1: Name the pattern

About 2 minutes

Write the pattern as specifically as you can. Name what is happening, for whom, and where. Avoid broad labels such as “achievement gaps” or “family engagement is low.”

See examples
  • Students from some groups are underrepresented in advanced coursework.
  • Discipline referrals are concentrated among particular student groups.
  • Some schools consistently attract and retain more experienced staff.
  • Families from some communities participate less often in formal decision-making spaces.

What is happening, for whom, and where.

Part 2: Look through three lenses

About 5 minutes

1

Policy and Structure

What rule, criterion, boundary, prerequisite, schedule, process, or long-standing practice helps shape this pattern today?

  • Who gains access most easily under the current structure?
  • Who has to navigate more barriers?
  • Which parts of the process feel normal mainly because they have been in place for a long time?
2

Place and Access

How might geography, transportation, feeder patterns, program location, housing patterns, or resource distribution affect who experiences this opportunity or outcome?

  • Where is the opportunity physically located?
  • Who can reach it easily?
  • What does a student or family need in order to take advantage of it?
3

People and Knowledge

Whose experience, expertise, history, or perspective shapes how your organization understands this pattern?

  • Whose explanation is treated as credible?
  • Who is usually consulted when the problem is discussed?
  • Whose knowledge might be missing?
